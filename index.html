Choose Your Direction

Pick one primary fitness goal.

You can always change your focus later. For now, choosing one main goal makes your training easier to understand and follow.

Build Glutes

Choose this if your main priority is developing stronger, fuller glutes.

  • Resistance training
  • Glute-focused exercises
  • Proper technique
  • Progressive overload
  • Recovery and consistent nutrition

Lose Body Fat

Choose this if reducing body fat is your main priority.

  • Strength training
  • Regular daily movement
  • Cardio when appropriate
  • Sustainable eating habits
  • Adequate protein and consistency

Get Stronger

Choose this if you want to improve strength and physical performance.

  • Resistance training
  • Proper form
  • Gradually increasing weight or reps
  • Tracking key lifts
  • Recovery and consistency

Body Recomposition

Choose this if you want to build muscle while gradually reducing body fat.

  • Consistent strength training
  • Progressive overload
  • Adequate protein
  • Balanced nutrition
  • Patience and recovery
Where You Train

Home, gym, or both can work.

The best training environment is the one you can follow consistently.

At Home

A strong option if you are new, value privacy, have a busy schedule, or do not currently have a gym membership.

  • Dumbbells
  • Resistance bands
  • Exercise mat
  • Bench or sturdy surface

At The Gym

Gym training gives you access to more equipment and makes it easier to increase resistance as you get stronger.

  • Dumbbells and barbells
  • Cable machines
  • Leg press and Smith machine
  • Cardio equipment

Use Both

You can train at the gym when your schedule allows and use home workouts when you need convenience.

  • Flexible scheduling
  • Fewer missed workouts
  • More ways to stay consistent
Know Your Baseline

Record where you are today.

Tracking is optional. The purpose is simply to give yourself something useful to compare against later—not to judge your starting point.

Helpful things to track

Use only the methods that feel useful to you. Consistency matters more than tracking everything.

Body WeightHelpful when viewed over time, not from one measurement.
Waist + HipsRetake measurements every few weeks under similar conditions.
Progress PhotosFront, side, and back in similar lighting and clothing.
Strength BenchmarksTrack exercises such as leg press, hip thrusts, dumbbell work, or push-ups.
Daily StepsUnderstand your current activity level before trying to change it.
Training ExperienceBeginner, intermediate, or experienced is enough as a starting reference.

Progress is more than the scale.

Strength, measurements, photos, energy, consistency, workout performance, and how your clothes fit can all help you understand progress.

Your starting point is simply your baseline. It is not a grade, and it does not need to be perfect.
